The satellite solar search and control system is used to achieve solar capture and attitude maintenance for the satellite. The system includes 9 gyroscopes, 4 solar sensors (two sets, primary and backup), 12 10N three-axis control thrusters, and a data management computer. The thrusters are grouped by axis to provide control torque for the roll, pitch, and yaw axes. The system receives ground commands via serial port, verifies them, and sets the satellite's operating mode. It periodically collects three-axis angular velocity data from the gyroscopes and solar azimuth and visibility data from the solar sensors. The angle signals are converted into 12-bit measurements via AD conversion to calculate the satellite's current three-axis attitude. The system controls the thrusters to output torque to drive the satellite's rotation. The entire search process is divided into four stages: a rate damping stage to reduce the three-axis angular velocity to zero to stabilize the attitude; a pitch search stage to control the satellite's rotation around the pitch axis to search for the sun; a roll search stage to control the satellite's rotation around the roll axis to search for the sun; and a solar cruise stage to maintain a stable attitude and continuously track the sun after capture. The system accumulates the runtime of the current mode and determines and switches operating modes based on preset conditions. The system operates on a 160ms control cycle, with timing driven by a 32ms timer interrupt. It communicates with the gyroscope via a serial port at a specified address, sending double-byte acquisition commands and receiving measurement data, with a byte transmission interval of less than 5 microseconds. Solar sensor switching is controlled by a 190ms positive pulse command with a pulse width of 1ms. The system has a fault handling mechanism: if the sun is not detected after two consecutive pitch and roll searches, it switches to the backup solar sensor and restarts the search; if the thruster's injection interval is less than 1 second for 5 consecutive seconds, it is considered a frequent injection fault and the corresponding thruster is shut down; if the gyroscope experiences communication errors for 5 consecutive cycles, a power-down reset procedure is executed; if the system cannot recover after the reset, it waits for ground commands. The system periodically collects the status and operating parameters of each component, packages them into telemetry data, and transmits it to the ground.
